# Trivi — a tiny app for quick questions

**$1**

---

This tiny app does **three things, and you can add more yourself**:

1. **Translate** a word into your languages — all at once.
2. **Explain** a concept, a phrase, or a topic in 3–6 short sentences.
3. **Answer** a quick question with just the answer (no fluff).

## How it looks

You type one word — for example, `dispatch` — and you immediately see translations into every language you've chosen, with example sentences:

**Translation works for any language.** In Settings you choose:

- **Source language** — the language you type in (e.g. English)
- **Target languages** — the languages you want answers in (e.g. German, Ukrainian — or French, Spanish, Polish, Japanese… any combination, as many as you want)

![Settings — Source and Target languages](3.png)

## Commands — short letters that tell the app what to do

Translation is the default — just type a word. For anything else, you start with a one-letter command and a space:

- **`e` — Explain.** Type `e black holes` → you get a 3–6 sentence explanation.
- **`q` — Question.** Type `q capital of Lithuania` → you get `Vilnius`. Just the answer.
- **`c` — Command.** (For coders) Type `c rename all .txt files to .md` → you get the exact terminal command.

You can also **add your own.** For example, the letter `s` for "summarise this text in 3 sentences", or `f` for "fix my grammar", or `p` for "make this more polite". Whatever you do often. You write the instruction once in Settings, and from then on a single letter does it.

![Extendable commands](4.png)

Think of commands like **shortcuts on your keyboard** — except instead of `Ctrl+C` to copy, it's `e ` to explain, `q ` to ask, `s ` to summarise. One letter, one job.
